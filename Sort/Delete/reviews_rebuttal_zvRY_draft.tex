\section*{Rebuttal, Reviewer zvRY}

We ran all three requested experiments. Together they isolate a factor we had
not separated in the submission: zero-shot preservation in CLIP continual
learning is a property of the anchor being \emph{frozen and pre-trained}, not of
distillation in general and not of the images the anchor is evaluated on.

\subsection*{Q3: is the frozen original CLIP important, or does any teacher work?}

We disabled the ZSCL reference stream so that RD is the only anchor in the run,
which is where the effect is measurable, and swapped only the RD teacher for the
checkpoint produced by task $t{-}1$, the classic LwF/iCaRL choice. Its caption
embeddings are re-derived from that checkpoint, so the target is that teacher's
own alignment distribution. The same setting also gives the image control of Q2,
so we report both from one set of runs.

\begin{table}[h]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\hline
\textbf{Configuration (no ZSCL branch)} & \textbf{Last} & \textbf{Avg} & \textbf{Transfer} \\
\hline
No anchor at all & 86.62 & 86.86 & 64.38 \\
RD on replay images, frozen teacher & 86.24 & 87.05 & \textbf{66.72} \\
RD on current-task images, frozen teacher & 86.71 & 86.70 & 66.66 \\
RD on replay images, previous-task teacher & 86.73 & 86.81 & 65.42 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent Against the frozen pre-trained teacher, a rolling self-teacher
recovers less than half of the available anchoring benefit and lands closer to
having no anchor at all. \textbf{The majority of RD's zero-shot preservation
comes specifically from the teacher being frozen and pre-trained}, and a
distillation signal on its own does not substitute for it. Prior methods vary
teacher and probe data together, so we are not aware of a controlled isolation
of this factor. It is consistent with the drifting-reference argument of
Sec.~2.3 and with LwF's behaviour in Table~2, but is now a direct test rather
than an inference.

\subsection*{Q2: are the replay images a core part of RD?}

Third row above, with only the probe images changed and the batch held at the
replay batch size so no arm receives a larger distillation batch. Current-task
images recover essentially all of the anchoring benefit, and Last is flat across
the block. The replay images are therefore not a core part of RD: it needs
in-domain probe images, not \emph{stored} ones, and we will drop the description
of the exemplars as serving two orthogonal roles within one minibatch, since
only $\mathcal{L}_\text{rep}$ requires them. This places RD on current-task
images at MulKI's design point and we will cite it there. Practically, the
anchoring term needs no reference image dataset and no buffer, which is a
setting where ZSCL cannot operate. Applying RD with the frozen teacher to
reference images is by construction $\mathcal{L}_\text{ZSCL}$, so that arm is a
re-weighting of the ZSCL term rather than an independent control; we state the
equivalence rather than reporting a run that rediscovers it.

\subsection*{Q1: when does RD help beyond standard replay?}

The $\lambda{=}0$ baseline below was re-run under ExRD's per-task iteration
schedule so that the two rows differ only in RD.

\begin{table}[h]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\hline
\textbf{Configuration} & \textbf{Last} & \textbf{Avg} & \textbf{Transfer} \\
\hline
ExRD, $\lambda{=}0.3$ & 86.17 & 87.15 & \textbf{68.37} \\
Replay only, $\lambda{=}0$ (iteration-matched) & \textbf{86.83} & 87.12 & 68.17 \\
ZSCL only, no replay & 83.79 & 87.12 & 68.69 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent On top of ZSCL, RD buys $0.20$ Transfer for $0.66$ Last. We therefore
present $\lambda$ as a Pareto knob rather than a uniform improvement, with
$\lambda{=}0$ as the Last-optimal endpoint and $\lambda{=}0.3$ as the
Transfer-favouring one. RD's distinct contribution is the anchoring quantified
above, where it replaces ZSCL's reference image stream entirely.

\begin{table}[h]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\hline
\textbf{Task} & \textbf{CLIP ZS} & \textbf{$\Delta$Last} & \textbf{$\Delta$Pre-exp.} \\
\hline
Aircraft      & 24.21 & $-0.33$ & n/a \\

EuroSAT       & 55.24 & $-2.17$ & $+0.13$ \\

ImageNet (held out) & 70.81 & $+0.37$ & $+0.20$ \\
\hline
mean (trained tasks) & & $-0.66$ & $+0.09$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\\[2pt]
{\small Pre-exp.\ is the mean accuracy on a task over the stages \emph{before}
it was trained, i.e.\ the per-task analogue of Transfer; it is undefined for
Aircraft, which is trained first, so the $\Delta$Pre-exp.\ mean is over the
remaining ten trained tasks. CLIP ZS is the measured zero-shot accuracy of the
pre-trained backbone, our proxy for proximity to pre-training.}
\end{table}

\noindent The trade is visible task by task rather than only in aggregate. The
cost in final accuracy is concentrated on a small number of tasks rather than
spread evenly across the sequence, while pre-exposure accuracy rises on most
eval sets, including held-out ImageNet. Splitting the tasks at the median
zero-shot accuracy, the gain is larger on the tasks closer to CLIP pre-training
than on the distant ones, which is the direction the reviewer suggested; at this
number of tasks the correlation is not statistically significant, so we report
it as suggestive rather than established.
